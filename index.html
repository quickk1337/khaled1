✦ Our Story

Born in the mud, raised with love.

Mooland Farms began with one stubborn Holstein named Beatrice and a dream bigger than the barn she slept in. Five generations later, we still wake up before dawn, still do things the slow way, and still believe the best things in life come from a cow.

Our herd roams over 800 acres of lush valley pasture, grazing on nothing but grass, sunshine, and the occasional wildflower. No shortcuts. No compromises. Just honest farming the way it was always meant to be done.

✦ A Day on the Farm

Life runs on moo time.

On a working cattle farm, every hour has purpose. Here's what a day at Mooland looks like from first light to last lantern.

04:30 AM
🌅First Milking

Before the rest of the world stirs, our dairy herd is already lined up at the parlour. The rhythm of the milking machines, the steam in the cold air, the warm smell of hay — there's nothing else like it.

06:00 AM
🌿Morning Turnout

The herd heads to pasture. Watching 240 cattle stream out across dewy grass as the sun comes up — that's our favourite moment of every day, without exception.

09:00 AM
🔧Maintenance & Vet Rounds

Fences checked, troughs cleaned, any animals needing attention seen to. A healthy herd is a happy herd — and our vet has known half these cows since they were calves.

03:30 PM
🍼Evening Milking

The dairy girls come home. Second milking of the day, followed by fresh bedding, feed supplements for the calves, and the quiet satisfaction of a day's work done right.

08:00 PM
🌙Night Watch

Calving season means someone's always on watch. There's a particular kind of magic in sitting with a new calf in a lantern-lit barn at midnight, knowing you've brought new life into the world.

Things you didn't know you needed to know.

27L
Daily milk production

A single Holstein can produce up to 27 litres of milk per day — enough to fill over 50 pint glasses. They're basically walking dairy factories with a personality.

360°
Vision range

Cows have nearly panoramic vision, seeing almost all the way around their heads. The only blind spot is directly behind them — which is why you should never sneak up on one.

8hrs
Daily chewing time

A cow spends up to 8 hours a day chewing cud — re-chewing partially digested food. It's meditative, really. They have four stomach chambers to get the job properly done.

200km
Smell detection range

Cows can smell things up to 10 kilometres away — and some studies suggest they can detect scents from even further. They know when rain is coming long before you do.

♫
Music lovers

Research has shown cows produce more milk when listening to slow, calm music — particularly classical pieces. Our herd has eclectic taste. Current favourite: Debussy's Clair de Lune.

40
Moos per day

Cows have distinct vocalisations for different situations — hunger, distress, greeting, or just saying hello. A mother cow and her calf can identify each other by voice alone within hours of birth.
